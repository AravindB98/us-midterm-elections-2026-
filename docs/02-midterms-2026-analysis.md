# 2. The 2026 Midterms: Analysis

[Back to README](../README.md) | Snapshot: **November 2, 2026**

## 1. What is at stake

- **House:** all 435 seats. Republicans hold a slim majority, about 218-214 with vacancies ([Northeastern Global News, Oct 12, 2026](https://news.northeastern.edu/2026/10/12/november-midterms-political-analysis/)). Majority = 218.
- **Senate:** 35 seats, including special elections in Florida and Ohio. Republicans hold 53-47 (the 47 includes two independents who caucus with Democrats); 23 of the 35 seats up are Republican-held, and Democrats need a net gain of four seats ([270toWin / Cook](https://www.270towin.com/2026-senate-election/cook-political-report-2026-senate)). A 50-50 split goes to Republicans through VP JD Vance.
- **Governors:** 36 races; 18 Democratic-held and 18 Republican-held seats are up, with 15 term-limited incumbents ([Election Central](https://www.uspresidentialelectionnews.com/2026-governor-elections/)). Nationally the GOP holds 26 governorships to 24 ([US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2026-10-28/2026-governor-race-ratings-democrats-gain-ground-in-key-states)).
- **Ballot measures:** voter ID, citizen-only voting, abortion, redistricting, taxes and more across dozens of states.

## 4. Senate: the path to 51

Democrats need **+4 net**. Cook's seven Toss Ups are Alaska, Iowa, Kansas, Maine, Michigan, Ohio and Texas; six of the seven are Republican-held, which is why Cook sees Democrats as slight favorites ([Newsweek on Cook](https://newsweek.com/forecast-shifts-14-races-towards-democrats-in-final-days-before-midterms-12640820)). Cook also moved North Carolina to Likely D and New Hampshire to Lean D ([Cook, Oct 6](https://www.cookpolitical.com/analysis/senate/senate-overview/senate-majority-squarely-democrats-sights-kansas-moves-toss-two)).

### Republican case

Republicans argue Kansas, Iowa, Texas, Ohio and Alaska all voted for Trump by double digits in 2024; NPR notes that Kansas's heavy GOP lean still makes it a harder flip and that Marshall's team expects to win ([NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/10/30/g-s1-149975/2026-midterms-senate-map)). Republicans also hope to pick up Michigan and hold Maine ([Cook](https://www.cookpolitical.com/analysis/senate/senate-overview/senate-majority-squarely-democrats-sights-kansas-moves-toss-two)), and a Fox News poll had Maine tied at 49% ([Fox News](https://www.foxnews.com/elections/2026/midterm-results/senate)).

### Democratic case

Final-week NYT/Siena polls show Democrats ahead in every key race, with Iowa essentially tied ([Political Wire](https://politicalwire.com/2026/11/01/democrats-lead-in-every-key-senate-race/)). Democrats have multiple combinations to reach +4 because most Toss Ups are GOP-held.

### Watch-outs

- **Alaska** and **Maine** use ranked-choice voting; if no one gets a majority, results can take days or weeks.
- **Nebraska**: independent Dan Osborn would not automatically caucus with either party, which is why Poliwave models an "independents decide" scenario.
- A uniform polling miss of 3 to 4 points toward Republicans would flip most Toss Ups back to the GOP.

## 5. House

- Cook says Democrats need only about 20% of its Toss Ups to reach a majority, assuming Lean/Likely D seats hold; Cook listed 21 Toss Ups in October ([Karmactive summary of Cook, Oct 9-10](https://www.karmactive.com/cook-political-report-18-house-race-ratings-democrats/)).
- Models put the median Democratic result around 230 to 241 seats (table above).
- **Redistricting** (Section 6) gives Republicans structural gains in Texas, Missouri, North Carolina and Ohio, partly offset by California's Prop 50 map.
- Bellwether districts to watch early on election night: Pennsylvania (Cook says it has the most Toss Ups, including PA-08), New York suburbs (NY-17), Iowa (IA-03), Arizona, Michigan, California's redrawn seats, and South Texas (TX-28, TX-34, TX-35).

## 7. Governors

Cook: Toss Ups are **Georgia, Kansas, Nevada**; Lean D includes Alaska, Iowa, Michigan, Ohio, Oregon and Wisconsin ([Cook](https://www.cookpolitical.com/ratings/governor-race-ratings)). DDHQ favors Democrats in 22 of 36 and has Nevada and Ohio as its only Toss Ups ([DDHQ](https://votes.decisiondeskhq.com/forecast/2026/governor)). US News says Democrats could hold a majority of governorships for the first time since 2010 ([US News](https://www.usnews.com/news/politics/articles/2026-10-28/2026-governor-race-ratings-democrats-gain-ground-in-key-states)). Full table: [docs/03](03-state-by-state-outlook.md#governors).

Why governors matter beyond their state: they sign or veto congressional maps, run (with secretaries of state) the 2028 presidential election, and set state policy on tuition, driver's licenses, and cooperation with federal immigration enforcement.

## 8. Ballot measures to watch

| Topic | States | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Ban noncitizen voting | Alaska, Arkansas, Colorado, Kansas, South Dakota, West Virginia | All but Alaska's would go into state constitutions |
| Voter ID + citizenship | Arizona, California | Combined measures |
| Voter ID only | Nevada (Q7), North Carolina, Ohio (Issue 3), Colorado (Amend. 84) | Nevada's needs a second approval after passing in 2024 |
| Abortion rights | Nevada (Q6), Idaho, Virginia | Establish a state right |
| Abortion restriction | Missouri (Amendment 3) | Would repeal the 2024 abortion-rights amendment and limit some gender procedures for minors |
| Redistricting | Missouri referendum | On the 2025 map |

Sources: [Daily Signal on SAVE-style measures](https://www.dailysignal.com/2026/10/18/congress-failed-but-voters-in-11-states-get-their-say-on-core-provision-of-the-save-act/), [The Federalist roundup](https://thefederalist.com/2026/10/07/heres-every-major-2026-ballot-initiative-you-need-to-know-about/), [OSV News](https://catholicreview.org/housing-gender-abortion-policies-among-state-ballot-measures-in-2026/), [Ballotpedia: Nevada](https://ballotpedia.org/Nevada_2026_ballot_measures), [WBUR Here & Now](https://www.wbur.org/hereandnow/2026/10/27/midterms-ballot-measures).

Federal context: the House passed the SAVE America Act (proof of citizenship to register, photo ID to vote) in February 2026, but it did not pass the Senate ([Gray DC](https://www.localnewslive.com/2026/02/12/house-passes-save-act-requiring-citizenship-proof-voter-registration/), [Daily Signal](https://www.dailysignal.com/2026/10/18/congress-failed-but-voters-in-11-states-get-their-say-on-core-provision-of-the-save-act/)). Bipartisan Policy Center notes noncitizen registration is rare: Utah's full-roll review found 27 confirmed cases among 2+ million registrants, with about 5,000 it could not confirm ([BPC](https://bipartisanpolicy.org/article/five-things-to-know-about-the-save-act/)).

## 10. Scenarios

| Scenario | Rough probability (model range) | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Democrats win both chambers | ~62-74% | Democratic Congress vs. Republican president: gridlock on legislation, aggressive oversight, judge confirmations get harder |
| Democratic House, GOP Senate | ~8-21% | House investigations; Senate keeps confirming nominees |
| Republicans keep both | ~8-15% | Administration agenda can move by majority in budget reconciliation |
| Independent decides Senate | ~1-8% | Nebraska outcome becomes pivotal |

Ranges combine DDHQ, Poliwave and midtermforecast.com joint-control estimates cited above.
