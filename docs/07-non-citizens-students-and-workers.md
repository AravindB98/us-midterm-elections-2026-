# 7. How the Election Affects Non-Citizens: Students and Workers

[Back to README](../README.md) | Snapshot: **November 2, 2026**

> Educational information, not legal advice. For your own case, talk to your school's Designated School Official (DSO) / international student office or a licensed immigration attorney.

## 7.1 The one rule that matters most: do not vote

Non-citizens, **including green card holders, F-1/J-1 students, people on OPT/STEM OPT, H-1B, L-1, O-1, H-4 and dependents**, cannot vote in federal or state elections.

| Law | What it covers | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| 18 U.S.C. 611 | Non-citizen voting in a federal election | Fine and up to 1 year in prison |
| 18 U.S.C. 1015(f) and 18 U.S.C. 911 | Falsely claiming US citizenship to register or vote | Up to 5 years (1015) / 3 years (911) |
| INA 212(a)(10)(D) | Unlawful voting | Inadmissible: visa or green card can be denied |
| INA 237(a)(6) | Unlawful voting | Deportable, often with a permanent bar; future naturalization can be denied |
| INA 212(a)(6)(C)(ii) | False claim of citizenship | Permanent inadmissibility in most cases |

This applies even if you were **registered by mistake**. Being on the rolls is a risk; casting a ballot is far more serious.

### Common traps

| Trap | What to do |
|---|---|
| DMV / RMV "motor voter" or automatic voter registration (many states, including Massachusetts) | When getting a license or state ID, answer citizenship questions truthfully and decline voter registration. If you later receive a voter card, do not use it; contact the local election office in writing to cancel and keep copies. Talk to your DSO or an attorney. |
| Campus or street registration drives | Politely decline. Do not check "I am a US citizen" on any form. |
| Friends saying "nobody checks" | States increasingly verify citizenship against DHS's SAVE database (e.g. Kansas checks twice a year, per [CAP](https://www.americanprogress.org/article/the-save-act-may-be-stalled-in-congress-but-state-versions-are-being-advanced-all-across-the-country/)). Records persist and resurface at green card or naturalization interviews. |
| Jury duty summons | Non-citizens are not eligible. Return the form marking non-citizen; do not ignore it. |
| Local elections that allow non-citizens (a few municipalities) | Rules are narrow, and several states are adding constitutional bans on non-citizen voting this November (AK, AR, CO, KS, SD, WV). Get advice before participating in anything. |

## 7.2 What non-citizens can legally do

According to the [FEC's guidance on foreign nationals](https://www.fec.gov/updates/foreign-nationals/) (a "foreign national" here means anyone who is not a citizen or green card holder):

| Activity | Allowed? |
|---|---|
| Discuss politics, follow the news, attend public rallies and debates | Yes |
| Volunteer (unpaid) for a campaign: door-knocking, phone banking, events | Yes, as an uncompensated volunteer ([MIT ISO](https://iso.mit.edu/common_questions/may-an-international-student-volunteer-in-a-u-s-election/)) |
| Make strategic or management decisions for a campaign or PAC | No |
| Donate money or in-kind goods to any federal, state or local candidate, party or PAC | **No** (52 U.S.C. 30121); green card holders are the exception |
| Paid campaign job | Only with valid work authorization, and still no decision-making role in the campaign |
| Encourage citizen friends to vote | Yes |
| Comment on proposed immigration rules | Yes, anyone can comment on [regulations.gov](https://www.regulations.gov/) |
| Contact members of Congress about policy | Yes |

Practical caution: in 2025 the administration revoked some international students' visas, citing reasons from driving infractions to protest activity ([University Daily Kansan](https://www.kansan.com/news/2026-midterm-elections-how-can-results-affect-international-students/article_d187da18-45a6-4c12-9c47-9853689668d7.html)). Keep status documents current, understand your rights, and ask your DSO before high-visibility activism.

## 7.3 Does Election Day change my status?

**No.** No F-1, CPT, OPT, STEM OPT or H-1B rule changes automatically because of the November 3 vote ([day1cpt.org](https://day1cpt.org/news/2026-midterms-h1b-green-card-policy), [GoElite](https://goelite.com/blogs/2026-midterms-h1b-green-card-impact)). What changes is **who controls Congress from January 3, 2027**, and therefore which immigration bills can pass, how agencies are funded and overseen, and which governors set state policy.

## 7.5 Scenario analysis: what each outcome could mean for you

This describes how political control typically shapes policy; it is not a prediction about any party's intentions.

| Outcome | Legislation | Executive rules | Oversight and funding |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Democrats win House and Senate** | Restrictive bills (e.g. ending OPT by statute) very unlikely to pass; pro-OPT or green-card bills could pass Congress but face a presidential veto | Administration can still issue rules and proclamations; courts remain the main check | Hearings on DHS/USCIS; appropriations fights; higher shutdown risk |
| **Split Congress** | Gridlock; little immigration legislation either way | Same as above | Investigations from the Democratic chamber |
| **Republicans keep both** | Restrictive H-1B/OPT bills get a friendlier floor; budget reconciliation can change fees/funding by simple majority | Rules proceed; courts still review | Agency funding more aligned with administration priorities |

In every scenario: **executive actions and court rulings move faster than Congress.** The OPT fee rule, duration-of-status rule and H-1B fee appeals will matter more in the next 6 months than the election itself.

### Shutdown watch

A budget standoff after January could again shut down parts of the government. In past shutdowns the Department of Labor's systems for Labor Condition Applications (needed for H-1B filings) and PERM paused, while USCIS (mostly fee-funded) kept running. Plan filing timelines with your employer.

### State-level effects

Governors and legislatures elected this week control in-state tuition rules, state driver's licenses and IDs, state scholarship eligibility, and how much state police cooperate with federal immigration enforcement.

## 7.6 Who is affected (scale)

- Indian nationals received about 71% of H-1B approvals in FY2024 (283,000+), and Brookings data indicates nearly 40% of initial H-1B approvals come from F-1 students/OPT ([Punjab Kesari summary](https://english.punjabkesari.com/us-news/h1b-visas-2026-midterms)).
- About 500,000 people are on OPT; nearly 30% of the ~331,000 Indian students in the US in 2023-24 were on OPT ([WION](https://www.wionews.com/world/green-cards-opt-h-1b-how-us-midterms-2026-could-reshape-indian-immigration-explained-1792755328619)).
- A survey cited by The PIE News found over half of current international students would not have enrolled if OPT had been rescinded ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/what-will-2026-bring-for-us-international-education/)).

## 7.7 Checklist for election week and after

- [ ] Do not vote, register, or check a citizenship box. If you are wrongly registered, get it cancelled in writing.
- [ ] Do not donate to candidates, parties or PACs.
- [ ] Volunteering is allowed only unpaid and without decision-making roles.
- [ ] Carry or have quick access to your I-20 / EAD / I-797 if traveling.
- [ ] Follow official sources for rule changes: [USCIS news](https://www.uscis.gov/newsroom), [Study in the States (SEVP)](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/), [Federal Register](https://www.federalregister.gov/), [State Dept visa news](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news.html).
- [ ] Track the OPT fee rule and duration-of-status implementation with your DSO.
- [ ] Northeastern students: [Office of Global Services](https://international.northeastern.edu/ogs/).
- [ ] Professional bodies: [NAFSA policy updates](https://www.nafsa.org/regulatory-information), [AILA (find an attorney)](https://www.ailalawyer.com/).
