Explainer

How US elections work, in six steps

Census

Every 10 years everyone living in the US is counted. That count sets how many of the 435 House seats each state gets.

Districts

States draw their own House districts. In 2025-26 several states redrew mid-decade (Texas, California, Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, Utah).

Primaries

Parties choose nominees between March and September, under rules that differ by state.

Voting

Citizens 18+ vote early, by mail or on Election Day. States and counties run elections, not Washington.

Counting

Networks "call" races; states certify official results later. Maine and Alaska use ranked-choice voting, Georgia holds runoffs.

Power

House majority = 218. Senate = 51 (or 50 + the Vice President). New Congress sworn in Jan 3, 2027.

For analysts

Methodology & open data

How the consensus is built

  • Inputs: forecast models, money-based prediction markets and crowd forecasts with a public Democratic-control probability.
  • Weights: models 1.0, markets 0.8, crowd 0.4. Aggregators are listed but not counted, to avoid double counting.
  • Freshness: values older than 14 days are shown but excluded. Unreadable sources keep their last value and are flagged "stale".
  • Cadence: markets every minute; headlines every 5 minutes; scraped models, race tables, poll table and charts every 15 minutes.
  • Simulator: race probabilities converted to expected margins (σ_total = √(σ_nat² + 4²)), with a shared national error term.
  • Limits: forecasts are probabilities, not results; lean labels are characterizations, not accuracy ratings.
