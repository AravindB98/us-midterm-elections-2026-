      1. Census

      Every 10 years the government counts everyone living in the US. That count decides how many of the 435 House seats each state gets.

      2. Districts

      Each state draws its own House districts. In 2025-26 several states redrew maps mid-decade (Texas, California, Missouri, North Carolina, Ohio, Utah).

      3. Primaries

      Parties pick their candidates between March and September. Rules differ by state.

      4. Voting

      Citizens 18+ vote early, by mail or on Election Day. States and counties run the elections, not the federal government.

      5. Counting

      TV networks “call” races from vote counts; official results come later, when each state certifies. Maine and Alaska use ranked-choice voting, so results can take longer.

      6. Power

      The House needs 218 seats for a majority, the Senate 51. The new Congress is sworn in on January 3, 2027.

      ✓ Non-citizens can

      • Follow and discuss the election, attend public events
      • Volunteer for a campaign without pay
      • Comment on proposed immigration rules at regulations.gov
      • Contact elected officials about policy

      ✗ Non-citizens cannot

      • Vote or register to vote in federal or state elections, even if registered by mistake at the DMV
      • Donate to any candidate, party or PAC (federal, state or local)
      • Claim US citizenship on any form
      • Make decisions for a campaign

      Unlawful voting can lead to criminal charges, visa denial and deportation. Source: FEC.

      Does Nov 3 change my visa?

      No. F-1, OPT, CPT and H-1B rules do not change automatically. The election decides who controls Congress from January 2027.

      Faster-moving items: the $100,000 H-1B fee (blocked in court), proposed OPT fees, the end of “duration of status”, and the wage-weighted H-1B lottery.
